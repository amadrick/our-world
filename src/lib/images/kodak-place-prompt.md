Create **one square image** for **[RESTAURANT NAME] in [CITY]**.

This image will appear as a thumbnail on a city recommendations map. Its first job is to make the place feel **specific, recognizable, and rooted in its actual location**. The image should capture what makes this restaurant visually memorable as a place—not just its logo, cuisine, or general vibe.

Before generating, identify the restaurant’s **2–4 most iconic physical characteristics** using reference imagery if available. These might include:
- its facade
- hanging sign or painted lettering
- awning
- entrance
- unusual windows
- corner location
- distinctive architecture
- bar or counter
- oven
- patio
- marquee
- tile, brick, woodwork, or other signature materials
- a characteristic relationship to the street or surrounding neighborhood

Choose the view that best communicates **“this is this place.”**

Favor the restaurant itself over generic food imagery. A viewer familiar with the restaurant should recognize it from the architecture, signage, spatial character, or defining physical details alone.

Do not invent a generic version of the restaurant. Preserve the important real-world relationships between its defining features even while stylizing them.

Visually, render the scene as a **stylized generative interpretation of a Kodak 35mm photograph**: grainy, hazy, imperfect, slightly dreamlike, and visibly image-made rather than documentary photography.

The image should feel like a memory of an actual place.

Use:
- pronounced but beautiful analog grain
- soft focus and slightly dissolved fine detail
- muted, imperfect color
- warm highlights
- subtle halation
- slightly irregular exposure
- gentle color casts
- imperfect texture and geometry
- restrained detail
- a little synthetic or painterly quality on close inspection

The image should sit between **photography, memory, and generative image-making**.

### Composition

Make the **place itself the subject**.

The restaurant should occupy most of the image. Show enough architectural context to communicate its physical identity, but remove unrelated visual clutter.

Prefer:
- a recognizable facade
- a characteristic corner or street-facing angle
- an iconic entrance or storefront
- a distinctive interior space when the interior is more recognizable than the exterior
- one strong architectural detail when it is genuinely synonymous with the place

Do not automatically center the logo or sign. Signage should support recognition, not substitute for a sense of place.

Preserve some sense of the restaurant’s relationship to its surroundings where that matters: the sidewalk, adjacent facade, corner, alley, neighborhood architecture, or other spatial cue that makes the location distinctive.

### Avoid

- generic restaurant imagery
- food as the primary subject
- photorealism
- pristine architectural photography
- real-estate photography
- glossy commercial photography
- HDR
- crisp digital sharpness
- excessive bokeh
- generic cinematic movie-still styling
- invented architectural features
- generic storefronts with the restaurant’s name pasted onto them
- film borders or sprocket holes
- fake Kodak branding
- captions or graphic-design framing
- collage
- clay or miniature aesthetics
- obvious 3D rendering

No decorative frame. No surrounding layout. **Output only the image.**

The final image should feel like **a visually heightened memory of the actual restaurant**: immediately grounded in its real architecture and defining physical details, but rendered through grain, softness, imperfect color, and subtle generative abstraction.
